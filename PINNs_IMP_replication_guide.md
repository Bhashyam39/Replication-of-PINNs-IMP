# Replication Guide: PINN for Metal Additive Manufacturing

**Source:** uploaded PDF, *“Machine learning for metal additive manufacturing: Predicting temperature and melt pool fluid dynamics using physics-informed neural networks”* by Zhu, Liu, and Yan. All equation, table, figure, and section numbers below refer to that PDF.

## 0. Bottom line

You can replicate this paper in two very different tiers:

1. **Feasible from the PDF alone:** the **1D aluminum/graphite solidification benchmark** in Section 3.1. It uses only the energy equation, has analytical solutions in Eqs. 23–26, and is the best first replication target.
2. **Not exactly replicable from the PDF alone:** the **3D NIST AM-Bench melt-pool results** in Section 3.2. The paper trains the PINN on labeled data generated by a high-fidelity FEM/VMS solver. The PDF does not provide that FEM dataset, the exact sampled labeled points, the trained weights, or enough optimizer settings for bit-for-bit reproduction.

Recommended strategy: **do not start with the 3D NIST case.** First reproduce the 1D solidification problem, including hard-vs-soft Dirichlet BC behavior. Only then attempt the NIST case, treating the FEM data as an external dependency you must recreate or approximate.

---

## 1. What the paper actually does

The paper builds a physics-informed neural network that maps space-time coordinates to field variables:

\[
[t,\mathbf{x}] \rightarrow [\mathbf{u}_{NN}, p_{NN}, T_{NN}]
\]

using a fully connected network with Swish activation. For metal AM, it embeds conservation of momentum, mass, and energy:

\[
r_M := \rho(\mathbf{u}_{,t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g})+\nabla p-2\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}
\]

\[
r_C := \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}
\]

\[
r_T := (\rho c_p T)_{,t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(\rho c_p T)+(\rho L f_L)_{,t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(\rho L f_L)-\kappa_T\nabla^2T-Q
\]

These correspond to Eqs. 1–3 and the residual definitions in Eq. 15.

The total loss is:

\[
L=(1-\lambda^1_{pde}-\lambda^2_{pde})L_{data}+\lambda^1_{pde}L^1_{pde}+\lambda^2_{pde}L^2_{pde}
\]

where:

- \(L_{data}\): mismatch against labeled \(\mathbf{u},p,T\) data, Eq. 14.
- \(L^1_{pde}\): interior PDE residuals \(r_M,r_C,r_T\), Eq. 20.
- \(L^2_{pde}\): Neumann traction and heat-flux residuals, Eq. 21.

### Key modeling assumptions

From Sections 2.1, 3.2, and 4:

- Solid and liquid have the same constant density in a given problem.
- Solid is treated as a highly viscous fluid.
- Melt-pool free surface is flat.
- Evaporation, ambient gas, composition change, and vaporization heat loss are neglected.
- Liquid fraction is linear in the mushy zone:

\[
f_L=
\begin{cases}
0, & T<T_s\\
(T-T_s)/(T_l-T_s), & T_s\le T\le T_l\\
1, & T>T_l
\end{cases}
\]

- Material properties are interpolated by:

\[
\psi=f_L\psi_L+(1-f_L)\psi_S
\]

## 2. Important corrections and missing details

Before coding, fix or explicitly handle these issues.

### Likely PDF/OCR errors

1. **Swish activation, Eq. 12.** The PDF appears to print \(x/(1-e^{-x})\). Standard Swish is:

\[
\sigma(x)=x\cdot \mathrm{sigmoid}(x)=\frac{x}{1+e^{-x}}
\]

Use the standard form.

2. **Hard-BC Heaviside function, Eq. 16.** As printed,

\[
H_\epsilon(x)=1-\cos(d(x)\pi/\epsilon),\quad d<\epsilon
\]

gives \(H_\epsilon\to2\) as \(d\to\epsilon^{-}\), then jumps to 1. The intended smooth ramp is almost certainly:

\[
H_\epsilon(d)=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1-\cos(\pi d/\epsilon)}{2}, & d<\epsilon\\
1, & d\ge\epsilon
\end{cases}
\]

so \(H=0\) on the boundary and \(H=1\) outside the artificial boundary layer.

3. **Momentum viscosity term.** Eq. 1 uses \(-2\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}\). For constant-viscosity incompressible flow, check this against the symmetric-stress form in Eq. 7 and Reference [30]. For a first replication, use the paper’s residual literally, but document the choice.

4. **Units.** Table 1 conductivity is likely \(W/(m\cdot K)\), not \(W/(kg\cdot K)\). Table 2 latent heat is garbled; use \(L=290\,kJ/kg\) for IN625.

5. **Energy latent advection term.** Eq. 3 and Eq. 15 are not perfectly consistent in the OCR. Use the conservative form from Eq. 3 unless you are deliberately testing the printed residual.

### Details the paper does not report

These are replication gaps:

- \(\lambda^1_{pde}\), \(\lambda^2_{pde}\)
- learning rate, Adam \(\beta_1,\beta_2\), learning-rate schedule
- batch size, number of iterations, stopping criterion, random seed
- input/output normalization
- collocation-point sampling distribution
- hard-BC thickness \(\epsilon\)
- exact number and placement of labeled FEM points for the NIST case
- whether one PINN was trained across Cases A/B/C or three separate PINNs were trained
- exact 3D geometry/scan origin and moving-frame convention
- TensorFlow version and trained checkpoints

Treat all choices for these as **assumptions**, not paper-faithful values.

## 3. Recommended staged replication path

### Stage 0 — Analytical 1D baseline

Implement the textbook solidification solution from Section 3.1 before any neural network.

Domain and initial state:

- \(x\in[-0.4,0.4]\) m
- graphite mold: \(-0.4\le x\le0\), \(T_{low}=298.15\) K
- liquid aluminum: \(0<x\le0.4\), \(T_{high}=973.15\) K
- aluminum melting temperature: \(T_{melt}=933.15\) K
- prediction window: \(t=5\) to \(10\) s

Material properties from Table 1:

| Material | \(\rho\) kg/m³ | \(c_p\) J/(kg·K) | \(\kappa\) W/(m·K) | Latent heat J/kg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Graphite | 2200 | 1700 | 100 | — |
| Al solid | 2555 | 1190 | 211 | 398000 |
| Al liquid | 2555 | 1190 | 91 | — |

Analytical interface:

\[
x^*(t)=7.095\times10^{-3}\sqrt{t}
\]

At \(t=10\) s:

\[
x^*(10)\approx 7.095\times10^{-3}\sqrt{10}\approx 0.02244\ \text{m}
\]

Acceptance checks:

- interface position at 10 s is approximately 22.44 mm
- far-left boundary tends toward 298.15 K
- far-right boundary tends toward 973.15 K
- temperature profiles visually match Eqs. 24–26

### Stage 1 — Replicate the 1D PINN

This is the minimum meaningful replication.

Network from Section 3.1:

- fully connected network
- 5 hidden layers
- 200 neurons per hidden layer
- Swish activation
- input: \((x,t)\)
- output: \(T(x,t)\)
- trained **without labeled field data**
- optimizer: Adam
- implementation: TensorFlow in the paper; PyTorch is acceptable for a replication if you state the substitution

Physics:

- use only the energy residual \(r_T\)
- impose initial condition at \(t=5\) s as data/initial constraint
- impose end temperatures using both:
  1. hard Dirichlet BC via corrected \(H_\epsilon\)
  2. soft Dirichlet BC via an added boundary loss

Hard BC construction:

\[
T_{NN}=T_{bc}[1-H_\epsilon(d)]+T_{raw}H_\epsilon(d)
\]

where \(d(x)\) is distance to the Dirichlet boundary and \(\epsilon\) is a small artificial thickness. The paper does not give \(\epsilon\); start with \(\epsilon\) equal to 1–5% of the relevant domain length and run a sensitivity study.

Suggested assumption set, clearly marked as non-paper values:

- Adam learning rate: \(10^{-3}\), optionally decay to \(10^{-4}\)
- collocation points: match the refinement study spirit using \(N_x=50,100,150,200\) equivalent resolutions over the \(x-t\) slab
- initial-condition points: dense enough to enforce the analytical initial temperature at \(t=5\) s
- loss weights: start with equal weighting, then tune only if training stalls
- normalize \(x\) and \(t\) to \([-1,1]\); consider scaling temperature by a reference such as \(T_{ref}=1000\) K

Validation targets:

- analytical interface position \(x^*(t)\)
- analytical temperature profiles at selected times
- hard-BC run should train faster and be more accurate than soft-BC run, reproducing the qualitative trend in Fig. 4
- PINN should remain accurate at low resolution better than standard linear FEM, reproducing the qualitative trend in Figs. 5–6

### Stage 2 — Build a simple FEM comparator

To reproduce Figs. 5–6, implement a basic 1D finite-element heat/solidification solver.

Required elements:

- linear elements
- resolutions \(N_x=50,100,150,200\)
- implicit or stabilized time integration
- apparent-capacity or enthalpy treatment for latent heat
- same material table and initial/boundary conditions as the PINN

Acceptance checks:

- FEM interface converges toward analytical \(x^*(t)\) as mesh refines
- FEM \(L_2\) temperature error decreases with refinement
- at low resolution, PINN error is visibly lower than FEM error, as claimed in Section 3.1

### Stage 3 — Reconstruct the NIST benchmark data

Only attempt this after Stages 0–2 work.

From Section 3.2 and Tables 2–3:

- material: IN625
- \(\rho=8440\) kg/m³
- \(T_s=1563\) K, \(T_l=1623\) K
- \(c_{p,s}=0.2441T+338.39\) J/(kg·K)
- \(c_{p,l}=709.25\) J/(kg·K)
- \(\kappa_s=3.0\times10^{-5}T^2-0.0366T+18.588\) W/(m·K)
- \(\kappa_l=30.078\) W/(m·K)
- \(L=290\) kJ/kg
- \(\mu=7\times10^{-3}\) Pa·s
- Marangoni coefficient \(\partial\gamma/\partial T=-2\times10^{-5}\) N/(m·K)
- reference temperature \(T_{ref}=295\) K

Laser model, Eq. 27:

\[
q_{laser}=\frac{2Q\eta}{\pi r_b^2}\exp\left(\frac{-2((x-V_st)^2+y^2)}{r_b^2}\right)
\]

with:

- absorptivity \(\eta=0.43\)
- beam radius \(r_b=50\,\mu m\)

Cases from Table 3:

| Case | Laser power | Scan speed |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 150 W | 0.4 m/s |
| B | 195 W | 0.8 m/s |
| C | 195 W | 1.2 m/s |

Caution: NIST AM-Bench metadata may distinguish commanded power from calibrated/actual power. If you download AM-Bench data, document exactly which power convention and track measurements you use.

Experimental comparison targets reported in the paper:

- melt-pool length: Case A \(659\pm21\,\mu m\), Case B \(782\pm21\,\mu m\), Case C \(754\pm46\,\mu m\)
- cooling rate: Case A \(6.20\times10^5\pm7.99\times10^4\) K/s, Case B \(9.35\times10^5\pm1.43\times10^5\) K/s, Case C \(1.28\times10^6\pm3.94\times10^5\) K/s

### Stage 4 — Generate or approximate FEM training data

The paper’s NIST PINN is not trained directly on sparse experiments. It is trained on labeled data from a validated FEM/VMS simulation.

Appendix details:

- residual-based variational multiscale formulation
- 4,464,276 tetrahedral elements
- local refinement around the moving laser
- fully coupled velocity/pressure/temperature solve
- generalized-\(\alpha\) time integration
- \(\Delta t=1\times10^{-6}\) s
- Newton linearization
- GMRES with block preconditioning
- 192 CPU processors on Stampede2
- FEM simulated to 2.0 ms
- PINN labeled data drawn only from \(t=1.2\) to \(1.5\) ms
- PINN predicts \(t=0\) to \(2.0\) ms

Practical options:

1. **Best fidelity:** reimplement the VMS thermal-fluid solver from the appendix and Reference [30]. This is a major CFD project.
2. **Pragmatic:** use an existing CFD/codebase that can produce comparable melt-pool \(u,p,T\) fields, then document the difference from the paper’s FEM.
3. **Replication-lite:** train the 3D PINN with PDE residuals plus only sparse experimental constraints. This tests the PINN idea but is not the paper’s exact NIST protocol.

Acceptance checks before PINN training:

- FEM mesh/time refinement changes melt-pool length by less than about 2%
- FEM Case B temperature profile has the same shape as Fig. 9
- FEM melt-pool lengths are close to Table 4 FEM rows: A 584.4 µm, B 743.6 µm, C 727.2 µm
- FEM cooling rates are close to Table 5 FEM rows: A \(8.00\times10^5\), B \(8.16\times10^5\), C \(1.38\times10^6\) K/s

### Stage 5 — Full 3D PINN

Network from Section 3.2:

- fully connected network
- 5 hidden layers
- 250 neurons per hidden layer
- Swish activation
- input: \((t,x,y,z)\)
- output: likely \((u,v,w,p,T)\)
- 788,651 collocation points for PDE residuals
- labeled data from FEM between 1.2 and 1.5 ms
- predict from 0 to 2.0 ms

Boundary conditions:

- top surface: moving Gaussian laser heat flux, Eq. 27
- top surface fluid traction: Marangoni condition, Eq. 28
- all other surfaces: no-slip velocity and fixed reference temperature
- flat top surface
- no gas phase, no evaporation, no free-surface deformation

Reported PINN targets:

Melt-pool dimensions, Table 4:

| Case | Length µm | Width µm | Depth µm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 594.8 | 193.3 | 64.0 |
| B | 740.3 | 160.0 | 52.8 |
| C | 732.5 | 131.5 | 43.2 |

Cooling rates, Table 5:

| Case | PINN cooling rate K/s |
|---|---:|
| A | \(8.54\times10^5\) |
| B | \(8.59\times10^5\) |
| C | \(1.38\times10^6\) |

Peak velocities:

- Case A: 1.641 m/s
- Case B: 1.566 m/s
- Case C: 1.446 m/s

Suggested acceptance criteria:

- melt-pool length within ±5% of Table 4 PINN values
- width/depth within ±5% of the FEM baseline if experimental width/depth are unavailable
- cooling rate within ±10% of Table 5
- peak velocity within ±10% of reported values
- stable extrapolation outside the 1.2–1.5 ms labeled window
- boundary-condition residual near hard-Dirichlet boundaries at numerical precision after normalization
- divergence residual small enough that contour and melt-pool metrics are insensitive to further residual reduction

Core PyTorch-style pseudocode for the 1D PINN:

```python
# Assumption-heavy skeleton; not paper-faithful hyperparameters.
T_raw = net(x, t)                      # normalized inputs recommended
H = hard_heaviside(distance_to_dirichlet_boundary(x), eps)
T_pred = T_bc * (1 - H) + T_raw * H    # exact Dirichlet enforcement

r_T = energy_residual(T_pred, x, t, material_props, latent_heat)
loss_pde = mean(r_T**2)
loss_ic = mean((T_pred(x, t=5) - T_initial(x))**2)
loss = w_pde * loss_pde + w_ic * loss_ic
```

Core residual pattern:

```python
T_t = autograd.grad(T_pred, t, create_graph=True)
T_x = autograd.grad(T_pred, x, create_graph=True)
T_xx = autograd.grad(T_x, x, create_graph=True)
f_L = liquid_fraction(T_pred, T_s, T_l)
rho_cp = interpolate(cp_s, cp_l, f_L)
kappa = interpolate(kappa_s, kappa_l, f_L)

r_T = (rho_cp * T_pred)_t - kappa * T_xx + latent_heat_term(f_L) - Q
```

For the 3D NIST case, extend this to outputs \((u,v,w,p,T)\), compute \(r_M,r_C,r_T\), add Neumann residuals for the top surface, and sample collocation points in the moving laser region densely enough to resolve the 50 µm beam radius.

## 5. Training pitfalls specific to this paper

1. **Latent heat is stiff.** The mushy-zone term can dominate the residual. Consider smoothing \(f_L\) slightly during early training, then reducing the smoothing, but label this as a deviation if the paper does not specify it.
2. **Hard BC needs a distance function.** For the 1D case this is easy: distance to left/right boundaries. For 3D NIST, define exact signed distances to each Dirichlet face.
3. **Scaling matters.** Temperatures are hundreds to thousands of K, velocities are m/s, and times are milliseconds. Normalize inputs and consider nondimensionalizing equations.
4. **Moving laser needs adaptive sampling.** Uniform sampling over 788,651 points may under-resolve the 50 µm beam. Use importance sampling around the laser path even if the paper does not specify it.
5. **Extrapolation is the hard part.** The PINN trains on FEM labels only from 1.2–1.5 ms but predicts 0–2.0 ms. Track residual and boundary errors outside the labeled window separately from labeled-data error.
6. **Cooling rate is more fragile than melt-pool length.** Table 5 errors are much larger than Table 4 errors. Do not treat melt-pool length agreement as proof that cooling rate will match.
7. **Do not overclaim exact replication.** Without the authors’ FEM data, code, seeds, and hyperparameters, exact numerical reproduction is not realistic. Aim for method-level replication and trend-level agreement.

## 6. Suggested first two-week plan

### Days 1–2
- Implement analytical 1D solution.
- Verify Table 1 properties and Eqs. 23–26.
- Write down corrected equations and assumptions.

### Days 3–5
- Implement 1D PINN: 5×200 FCNN, Swish, Adam, hard and soft BC modes.
- Normalize \(x,t,T\).
- Train without labeled field data.

### Days 6–8
- Run hard-vs-soft BC comparison.
- Run collocation refinement study.
- Compare interface history and temperature \(L_2\) error against analytical solution.

### Days 9–11
- Implement simple 1D FEM comparator.
- Reproduce qualitative FEM/PINN convergence behavior.

### Days 12–14
- Write a replication report: what matched, what did not, which settings were assumed, and which results are sensitive to those assumptions.
- Only then scope the 3D NIST replication and decide whether to recreate FEM data.

## 7. What I would count as a successful replication

A strong replication does not need to match every digit in Tables 4–5. It should show:

1. **1D benchmark:** hard BC outperforms soft BC; interface position and temperature errors converge toward analytical values; low-resolution PINN beats low-resolution FEM qualitatively.
2. **NIST trend:** increasing scan speed produces longer, shallower, narrower melt pools; cooling rate increases from Case A to Case C; Case B temperature profile follows the experimental trend.
3. **PINN advantage:** moderate labeled data plus PDE residuals outperform a data-only neural network trained on the same labels.
4. **Error honesty:** all assumed hyperparameters, corrected equations, and external data dependencies are documented.

If you want, the next concrete step is to implement **Stage 0 + Stage 1** as a runnable 1D replication package.
